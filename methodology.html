Review Methodology

How I test, what I measure, and why my ratings matter

The Short Version: I buy everything with my own money, use it for weeks/months before reviewing, and tell you exactly what I think - good or bad. No sponsors. No affiliate links. No pressure to be nice.

Core Principles

1. I Buy Everything Myself

Every product I review is purchased with my own money. No free samples, no "review units" from manufacturers. When I spend $200+ on something, you bet I'm going to be honest about whether it was worth it.

2. Real-World Testing, Not Lab Conditions

I don't have a testing lab. I have a life. Products get tested the way you'd actually use them - on trails, at theme parks, during commutes, in bad weather. That's more valuable than controlled benchmarks.

3. Long-Term Use When Possible

Most "reviews" happen within days of receiving a product. I try to use things for weeks or months before reviewing. Boots get reviewed after 100+ miles. Electronics after real-world battery cycles. First impressions lie.

4. Zero Financial Incentive to Be Nice

No affiliate links. No sponsorships. No brand partnerships. If I trash a product, I don't lose money. If I praise one, I don't make money. My only incentive is to help you make better decisions.

My Rating Scale

I use a 5-star system, but here's what each rating actually means:

★★★★★

5 Stars - Exceptional

Rare. This product exceeded expectations. I'd recommend it without hesitation. No major flaws for its intended purpose.

★★★★☆

4 Stars - Great

Solid product with minor issues. Good value for money. Would recommend to most people.

★★★☆☆

3 Stars - Average

Does its job, but nothing special. Has noticeable flaws. Might work for some use cases.

★★☆☆☆

2 Stars - Disappointing

Significant problems. Not worth the money for most people. Might work if you have very specific, limited needs.

★☆☆☆☆

1 Star - Avoid

Fundamentally broken or misleading. Don't buy this. I regret wasting my money.

What I Test For

Product Reviews

  • Build quality: Will it last? Does it feel cheap?
  • Performance: Does it do what it claims?
  • Value: Is it worth the price compared to alternatives?
  • Real-world usability: How does it perform outside perfect conditions?
  • Long-term durability: Does it hold up over time?

Experience Reviews (Theme Parks, Activities)

  • Intensity: How extreme is it really?
  • Value: Was the cost justified by the experience?
  • Crowds/Wait times: What's the realistic expectation?
  • Authenticity: Does the marketing match reality?
  • Reride factor: Would I do it again?

What Makes Me Different

Most reviewers: Get free products → Need to maintain relationships with brands → Give 4-5 stars to everything → Make money from affiliate links when you buy

Me: Buy my own stuff → Owe nobody anything → Give honest ratings → Make zero money from your purchases

I'm not saying sponsored reviewers are all liars. But when your income depends on staying in a brand's good graces, there's inherent pressure - conscious or not - to soften criticism. I've removed that pressure entirely.
